# Podcast episode: writing guide

- Write for the language you are given. Open the episode by introducing the hosts, and the narrator if there is one, as AI voices.
- Follow the series' segments and their target minutes (about 150 spoken words per minute). No new research.
- **News recap:** a conversation between the hosts retelling the week's published stories.
- **Story:** tell one story as a narrative.
  - Name its protagonists first (one to three): the people the story follows, each grounded in claims.
  - Build the arc from beats, in this order: `cold-open` (a vivid moment or question, no context yet), `context`, `turning-point`, `stakes`, `resolution`, `takeaway`. `stakes` may come before `turning-point`; every other beat keeps its place. All six appear once.
  - The arc follows the protagonists: the turning point and the resolution are about what they did or what happened to them.
  - The narrator sets scenes and moves between beats; the hosts react, explain and argue. Keep narrator lines short.
  - Add a `direction` only where delivery changes meaning ("hushed", "dry", "faster"); most lines need none.
  - Use sound cues sparingly: a `sting` after the cold open, `transition` between beats where the mood shifts, a short `pause` before the takeaway.
- Paraphrase sources; never read a quote aloud as a quote. Attribute in speech ("the paper argues…").
- Every factual line lists the claim ids it relies on. Dramatise the telling, never the facts: no invented scenes, dialogue or feelings.
