# Decisions

Every settled decision, with a one-line reason. A new decision gets the next number; a changed decision is struck through and replaced by a new entry that names it. Undecided questions are tracked as GitHub issues, never here.

## IPTC standards

| #   | Decision | Reason |
| --- | -------- | ------ |
| D40 | **Digital source type.** A publication is `trainedAlgorithmicMedia`; once the audit log shows an editor changed its text it becomes `compositeSynthetic` (label: "AI-made, edited by a person"). Replaces D26. | IPTC defines `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia` as human media edited with generative AI, the reverse of ANR's case; `compositeSynthetic` is a mix of elements with at least one AI-made. |
| D41 | **Subjects are IPTC Media Topics.** Each beat in `policy.yaml` lists one or more Media Topics; the summariser places a lead in beats, and a publication carries its beat's topics. | Standard subject codes come from configuration, so no model invents a code. |
| D42 | **Genre per format.** `format.yaml` names one IPTC genre (newsletter issue `genre:Summary`, classic article and podcast episode `genre:Feature`, artist story `genre:Profile`); an opinion item adds `genre:Opinion`. | Readers and machines can tell a briefing from a profile from opinion. |
| D43 | **ninjs 3.2 out.** Every published publication has a ninjs 3.2 twin at its page URL plus `.json`, with subjects, genres, digital source type and trust indicators. Leads keep the fields of a ninjs item. | IPTC's JSON news format is the syndication format; leads are exportable the same way. |
| D44 | **Reader pages carry schema.org JSON-LD** (`NewsArticle` or `PodcastEpisode`, `digitalSourceType`, `about`, `genre`, `inLanguage`; publisher a `NewsMediaOrganization` with its policy links). No HTML meta tag for the source type. | schema.org's `digitalSourceType` takes IPTC's values; no standard meta tag exists. |
| D45 | **Images use IPTC Photo Metadata.** Ingestion reads creator, credit line, copyright notice, web statement of rights, licensor and digital source type from XMP; the publisher writes them back into every published image, next to its C2PA manifest. An image keeps its own recorded source type, never the publication's, and an unknown one stays unset. | Credit and licence travel with the file. |
| D46 | **Vocabularies are vendored** in `packages/schemas/iptc/` (NewsCodes under CC BY 4.0, and the ninjs 3.2 JSON Schema), refreshed by hand; config validation rejects unknown and retired codes. Reader-facing names in fa, en and tr are ANR's own. | CI stays offline and reproducible; IPTC labels exist only in English. |

## Ingestion

| #   | Decision | Reason |
| --- | -------- | ------ |
| D55 | **Raw pages** are stored content-addressed (`raw/<sha256>`): in a local directory while ANR runs on the laptop, in R2 from milestone 7 (`ANR_RAW_BUCKET`). A lead keeps the key of the page as fetched. | The same bytes are stored once, and a key always names one frozen snapshot. |
| D56 | **Near-duplicates** are found in two stages: leads of the last 30 days whose 64-bit SimHash of word 3-shingles is within 12 bits, then confirmed when the Jaccard overlap of their shingles is at least 0.8. Texts under 24 shingles get no SimHash. | SimHash alone is noisy on short posts (a one-line intro moved a copy 8 bits; unrelated texts sit near 32); the exact overlap decides. No extra index service. |
| D57 | **Lead sightings.** Every URL a lead was seen at is a row (`lead_sightings`: original, same URI, same text, near-duplicate), with its source. A copy never becomes a second lead. | Source independence is counted from one lead and its sightings, so syndication never looks like corroboration. |
| D58 | **Fetching.** One client for every request: http and https only; the host resolved at connect time, every answer required public, and the connection made to a checked address (IPv4 first, then each in turn); each redirect hop checked again (at most 5); address literals refused before any transport; robots.txt per origin (RFC 9309: 4xx no rules, 5xx or unreachable nothing, asked again after 5 minutes), its `Crawl-delay` the host's minimum gap; otherwise 1 s between requests to a host (3 s for arXiv's API); 10 MB per response; pages decoded in their declared charset; at most 50 new leads per scheduled run. A listing's ETag and Last-Modified are kept only when every item was handled (none left by the cap or a failed fetch other than 4xx), so the next run takes the rest. An answer that cannot be read counts as a failed fetch of the source. The `ingest-source` job expires 5 minutes after its 15-minute HTTP call times out, so one source never has two runs at once. | Ingestion is the only part of ANR that touches the web; an injected URL can reach neither a private network nor a site's disallowed pages. |
| D59 | **Paper tier rules.** An arXiv paper is flagged and not stored when withdrawn; low (tier 3) when its abstract is under 200 characters or it is only cross-listed into the source's categories; high (tier 1) only when Semantic Scholar shows a venue on `papers.high_venues` or at least `papers.high_citations` citations; otherwise medium (tier 2). Each arXiv run re-checks its papers of the last 30 days and only raises tiers, and only once the Semantic Scholar source has `terms_checked`. | A paper earns tier 1 from independent evidence of review or impact, never from its authors' own comment. |
| D60 | **Hacker News** items are stored as metadata only: the lead is the discussion page (title, text, points), and the linked article is a ninjs link that is never fetched by the scheduled run. | Linked sites are arbitrary and their terms unread (D17); an agent can still request a page, at tier 3. |
| D61 | **Source requests** run the same pipeline at once and return the lead. A URL takes a source's tier only inside that source's section (its host and first path segment, from the source URL or a lead it gave), and only if the source's terms are checked; elsewhere on a site with an unchecked source it is refused; anything else is tier 3, and so is a vouched URL that redirects out of its section. At most 5 fetches per agent run, logged in `source_requests` before each fetch, whatever the fetch turns out to be. | An injected page cannot promote itself, not even on a shared host such as `huggingface.co`, and a run cannot turn into a crawl. |
| D62 | **Ingestion contract.** The agents worker calls the ingestion service over internal HTTP with bodies defined as JSON Schemas in `packages/schemas` (`ingest-run`, `ingest-result`, `source-request`, `source-request-result`). 404 and 422 (unknown, or never to be fetched) end the job; 502 and other failures are retried by pg-boss. | One definition for both languages; a refusal needs a person, a failure needs only time. |
| D63 | **Summariser.** One run per new lead: an Agent run row and its `agent-run` job are written in one transaction. The model answers in plain JSON; code validates it (summary of at most 400 characters in the lead's language, beats only from the newsroom's live slugs), with one retry carrying the errors, then outcome `invalid`. A valid answer is stored with the run's outcome and tokens in one transaction; a finished run is never rerun, and a lead has at most one summariser run (a unique index). Without a model key the worker does not take summariser jobs, so they wait; at start and after each ingest run it re-queues runs that have no job and leads with no run, and closes as `error` a run whose job used up its retries. At most 20,000 characters of the lead are sent. Cost stays empty until milestone 3's budgets. | Code, not the model, decides what counts as a beat, and no provider-specific structured-output feature is needed. |
| D64 | **API sources and robots.txt.** An `api` source's own requests (its listing URL, and per-item calls such as Hacker News items) follow the API's terms of use, read before `terms_checked`, and its rate limits; robots.txt is not consulted for them. Every page fetch still honours robots.txt. Amends D58. | robots.txt (RFC 9309) speaks to crawlers; an API is an explicit invitation with its own terms. arXiv's API host disallows every crawler while its API documentation directs programs to it. |
| D65 | **Beat descriptions.** Each beat in `policy.yaml` may carry a one-line `description` (stored on the beat); the summariser sees it next to the slug and the beat's Media Topic labels. | Beats of one newsroom often share their Media Topics (every AI beat is "artificial intelligence"), so labels alone cannot tell them apart. |
| D66 | **Model call pacing.** The agents worker spaces summariser calls by `ANR_SUMMARISER_RPM` (default 10 a minute). A rate-limit answer (429) never uses a pg-boss retry: the job is queued again after the provider's suggested delay, or an hour for a daily quota, and every other call waits meanwhile. LangChain's own retries are off. | Provider limits depend on the API key's tier, so the rate is a deployment setting; a free-tier key stays within its quota without losing summaries. |
| D67 | **A copy from a better source credits the lead.** When a source with a better tier sees a stored lead (same URI, same text or near-duplicate), the lead takes that tier and source; its text stays frozen and every sighting is kept. arXiv papers are never raised by a copy: their tier follows D59. | Which source happens to run first must not decide a lead's tier. |
| D68 | **Age limit.** A newsroom's `policy.leads.max_age_days` bounds scheduled runs: an item older than that by its newest date (published or updated) is not stored, checked before its page is fetched when the listing dates it and after extraction otherwise; an undated item is not stored. An agent's request has no age limit. The AI newsroom uses 14 days. | A source's first run must not backfill years of posts into the pool, or spend a summary on each. |
| D69 | **Listing pages keep articles only.** A link from a `site` listing is stored only if its page says it is an article: `og:type` article, or JSON-LD of an Article type (Article, NewsArticle, BlogPosting and their kinds). Feed items and agents' requests are not filtered: a feed lists items, and an agent asks for a page on purpose. | A listing links its category and tag pages too, and the path alone cannot tell them from posts. |
| D70 | **Feed-only sources.** A source with `pages: false` stores each feed item's own title and text as the lead and never fetches the item's page. | For terms that allow a publisher's feed but ban robots on its site. |
| D71 | **Fact-checker model family.** Every catalog model has a `family` (the weights, whoever serves them). The fact-checker's chain drops every family in the story's desk Agent runs and tries the rest in order; config validation requires at least two families in it. Replaces D22. | The same weights served by two providers are the same writer; a run that fell back may have used several. |
| D72 | **Model chains and free development models.** Each role names an ordered chain of catalog models; `@anr/models` calls it and falls through on rate limits, outages and retired models, never on a bad key or request, and holds the run when the chain is exhausted. `ANR_MODELS=free` selects `config/models.free.yaml` (Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter, Mistral, NVIDIA NIM free tiers) for development; deploy refuses it. | Development costs nothing while no single free tier is enough; free tiers may train on prompts and promise nothing. |

## Desk and gate

| #   | Decision | Reason |
| --- | -------- | ------ |
| D73 | **Agent tools.** `read_lead` returns a lead's frozen text (cut at 12,000 characters), publisher, tier, beats and any withdrawn mark; `search_leads` ranks the newsroom's leads by `pg_trgm` word similarity of the query to `anr.normalize_text()` of headline, summary and text (a GIN index on that expression), then tier and recency, never returning withdrawn leads; `request_source` calls ingestion's `/request-source` (D61) and answers every refusal as text. Models are bound to these three; code calls `submit` with a validated draft. A test pins the four names. | Word similarity, not similarity, because a query is short and a lead long; a model passing a whole draft as tool arguments is the least reliable thing free models do. |
| D74 | **Corroboration.** A claim may list up to four further sources, `also: [{lead_id, quoted_passage}]`, stored in `claim_sources`. Each passage is quote-matched like the first; independence counts the publishers (source publisher, else the URL's host) of the claim's sources whose passage matched. | A claim has one lead, and a syndicated copy is never a second source (D57), so a non-tier-1 fact had no way to show two. |
| D75 | **Prompt assembly.** Platform role prompt, the newsroom `style.md`'s "All languages" section and the target language's section, and the format `guide.md` (a newsroom may override the guide). At most 5 KB in UTF-8 bytes, including the desk's step prompts; `prompt_version` is the role and a hash of the assembled text. A test covers every newsroom, series format and language. | Persian is two bytes a letter; a content hash versions every part at once. |
| D76 | **Budgets.** `format.yaml` `run_tokens` caps input plus output tokens over a run's model calls; the run ends there and its publications are held. `policy.yaml` `budget: {monthly_usd, check_reserve_usd}` caps spend per calendar month in the newsroom's timezone over every Agent run (summaries included; the summariser records its cost from now on, D63), counting what interrupted attempts spent: writing roles (planner, desk, renderer, summariser) do not start at `monthly_usd - check_reserve_usd`, the fact-checker and item matcher not at `monthly_usd`; a run that started may finish. A `notices` row tells the Editor-in-Chief at 80% and at the writing limit, once a month each. A desk or renderer run that cannot start is `paused` and its publications held. | The reserve is money only checks can spend, so a draft written before the cap is still checked; the token cap bounds what a started run can add. |
| D77 | **Desk and renderer.** The desk graph is read (code: the assignment's leads, most credible first, each with its summary and the first 1,500 characters of its text) → outline → draft → claims → validate, research tools bound at every model step (six tool rounds a step). Validation is the format's zod schema plus what it cannot express: claims cite only leads given or read in the run, none withdrawn, each passage found after normalisation and at least 20 characters, the story's beat live and not banned, every item listing known claims. One retry with the errors; a second failure holds every publication of the assignment. Claims are written in their lead's language, the story in the newsroom's primary language; a renderer run per other series language writes the same items with the same claim ids and no tools. A submit closes the run, stores story, claims (`ref` c1, c2…), sources and rendition, marks the publication drafted and queues the renderers, then the gate, in one transaction. | Same-language claim and passage make fact-checking a comparison, not a translation; the gate needs every language, and other formats and podcast scripts wait for milestone 5's renderer. |
| D78 | **Withdrawn leads** (issue #3). A stored lead later found withdrawn keeps its frozen text and gets `withdrawn_at` and a reason: search and assignments skip it, the desk cannot cite it, and the verifier fails a claim that does. Each arXiv run asks arXiv for its record of the source's papers of the last 30 days (`id_list`, 50 a request) and marks those it now calls withdrawn, as does a listing that shows a stored paper withdrawn. Retraction data and a venue deny list are not part of this (issue #3). | Claims may already cite the lead, so it can neither change nor disappear; lowering its tier would let it keep corroborating. |
| D79 | **Verifier code checks** run in the Python verifier service (`POST /verify`, JSON Schemas `verify-request` and `verify-result`), which reads the story with `with_newsroom` and stores every result as a Check in one transaction: rendition schema; quote match (shared normaliser, then whitespace collapsed, at least 20 characters); withdrawn leads; source independence; opinion where policy forbids it; beats (the story's and every cited lead's: banned or not yet placed fails, restricted is listed); item–claim consistency (every item lists known claims; its numbers, after folding Persian and Arabic digits and separators, and its Latin-script names are in its claims' text or passages); outside-article leads exist; style lint per language (Arabic ye and kaf and a detached می in Persian, a Persian rendition mostly in Persian script, no Arabic script in Latin-script languages, English hype words, double spaces); and `checks.yaml` rules by id, their numbers read from the rule text. An unknown rule id is an error. The disclosure check renders each rendition through every channel template its series uses for that language with `@anr/render`, the publisher's own renderer, and requires the label. | A story-wide result is stored once per rendition so each publication shows it; the disclosure check must see exactly what would be sent. |
| D80 | **Gate and policy.** A `gate` job per story is queued when its last language is written (by the desk itself for a one-language series), on a `stately` queue keyed by story, so one gate per story runs at a time: code checks, disclosure, then, only if every code check passed, the fact-checker (one fresh call per fact claim and passage, each check recording which source it covers, its chain without any family of the story's desk and renderer runs) and the item matcher (one call per item with its claims), storing each answer as it comes; a call no model answers is an error or timed-out check. Policy then re-reads each publication under a row lock, leaves any that is no longer drafted, and passes one only when every check it needs exists and passed; it goes on to `scheduled` only as daily news in an `auto_publish` format with no restricted beat; anything else is held (`with-editor`) with policy Checks naming why. The agents worker holds every publication still assigned or drafted 30 minutes before its slot. | A check that never ran leaves no failure behind; requiring each expected check makes silence a hold, never an approval. |
| D81 | **Daily assignment.** At a daily series' `assignment_at`, its slot is that day's cadence time in the newsroom's timezone (a passed slot is skipped); its leads are those stored in the last 24 hours, summarised, not withdrawn, in no banned beat and not used by the series before, ordered by tier then date, at most two per item the series allows (24 at most). One transaction writes the assignment, its leads, one publication per series language and the desk run with its job; no leads writes a `no-leads` notice instead. | A summary means the lead's beats are known, so a banned one is never assigned. |
| D82 | **Prices.** Every model a role or TTS uses needs a `price` in its models file; config validation rejects one without. Paid prices come from the provider's published price list, cited in the file. | A budget cannot count what it cannot price. |
| D83 | **Too large for one model.** A 413 (a request over a model's own limit, such as a free tier's tokens per minute) moves the call to the next model in the chain with no cooldown; it is not a bad request. Amends D72. | A desk prompt that one free tier refuses fits another, and smaller calls still fit the first. |
| D84 | **Local models in development.** A models file may name an OpenAI-compatible server on this machine (plain `http` to `127.0.0.1` or `localhost`, no key, its own `timeout_s`); config validation keeps such a provider out of `models.yaml`. The free profile tries LM Studio first: `openai/gpt-oss-20b` writes (desk, renderer), `google/gemma-4-12b` checks (fact-checker, item matcher), both with a 64k context, sized for a 36 GB laptop. A 400 over a model's context window counts as too large (D83), and "model unloaded" as an outage. | Free cloud tiers were overloaded or too small for the desk's prompt; a laptop's own models have no rate limits and keep prompts local. Gemma 4 12B could not produce the desk's claims step (it repeated its draft twice); one claim or item at a time against one passage suits it. |
| D85 | **Output cap and reasoning effort.** One model call may write at most 8,192 tokens, thinking included. A model in a models file may set `reasoning_effort` (`none`, `low`, `medium`, `high`), sent as is to an OpenAI-compatible provider; the free profile turns local Gemma's thinking off. | With thinking on, Gemma 4 12B spent 13–18k tokens reasoning per desk call and answered in 3, so every call hit its 10-minute timeout.
